# audit-kit — verify a priced, receipted weight change without trusting the authors

This kit lets you check, on a laptop, the two results below from files whose hashes you verify first. What the kit establishes and what it does not is stated plainly in "What the checks do and do not establish" below; read that section before the results.

**Result 1 (FP-678).** On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, installing a 60-lesson invented skill ("vex", a made-up list library graded by executing the model's code), one windowed, projected weight write (113 optimizer steps over layers 28-35 with replay, i.e. a constrained fine-tune, not a single-step edit) passed three pre-registered screens at once — the skill taught (60/60), a held capability kept (HumanEval+ 112/164, equal to the base) and 641 protected facts held within the price screen (bill 30.6 nats of summed margin loss, 0 sign crossings; the same write also gained 28.5 nats of margin elsewhere on the panel, so "kept" means no fact flipped and the loss stayed under the line, not that the margins were unchanged). The two-family artifact px3 was also read on 641 fresh bank facts that no span was built from (FP-700, the fourth table): bill 31.0 nats with 1 crossing out of span against 33.8 with 1 on the protected panel, so the price transfers to unseen facts at this size — while three published editors at their published settings installed nothing (AlphaEdit 0/60 and broke the model; WISE 1/60; GRACE 5/60) and plain LoRA and full fine-tuning installed everything and failed the price (LoRA: HumanEval+ 32, bill 155; full: 102, bill 74). The unguarded version of our write (U_vex: no projection) is also in the table: 60/60, HumanEval+ 107, bill 36.8.

**Result 2 (FP-682b, FP-682c).** A second invented skill ("quill", a string library) was added on top of the first by repeating its lessons under rehearsal of the first skill's graded pairs, with each step projected out of the protected facts' activation span. Two lineages, two software stacks, both inside all three screens at the third pass: pq3 (vex 60, quill 57, HumanEval+ 111 against a same-stack base of 117, bill 48.3 nats / 1 crossing) and px3 (vex 60, quill 57, HumanEval+ 109 against a base of 112, bill 33.8 / 1 crossing). The earlier rungs are in the tables too and are NOT two-family artifacts (px1: quill 3/60; pq2: HumanEval+ 107, below the line). Held-out language-modelling loss on 200 pretraining-like documents (FP-688, a descriptive fourth measure: no threshold was set before the read, so it is not a screen) moved by +0.001 (px3; worse on 116 of 200 documents) and +0.004 (pq3; 153 of 200) nats; the unprojected lineage on the same box paid +0.010 to +0.017 (179-185 of 200) and the 1.5B ladder +0.030 (192 of 200) — all six arms are in the kit. The unguarded head-to-head write U_vex also passes all three screens on its own (60/60, 107, 36.8/0); what the projection buys on that panel is the subject of FP-682d (panel span versus a random span of equal rank), running at release time; its visible effect in this kit is in the two-family ladder and the fourth measure.

## The three screens (why these)
- **Teach**: the 60 taught tasks of the skill, graded by executing the generated code (at least 54 to pass). Held-out tasks (60 new pipelines, same template) are reported, not screened.
- **Plus**: HumanEval+ pass@1 on 164 problems, a capability the base already had; pass if within 6 of the base read the same way on the same stack. On the 5090 stack the base is 112 (line 106); on the box-2 stack the base is 117 (line 111). Disclosure: the FP-682b pre-registration froze its plus line at 108, inherited from the FP-677 pre-registration, before the box-2 base was re-read at 117; pq3 (111) clears both lines and pq2 (107) fails both, so no verdict depends on the choice.
- **Bill**: the summed margin loss over 641 held facts (the model's log-probability gap between the right answer and the best wrong one), against the base read in the same process; pass if at most 53.12 nats with at most 2 sign crossings (facts that flipped to wrong). Units: nats and counts throughout.

## Layout
Everything checked lives under `evidence/`, including the two files the trust story depends on, `evidence/bundle.json` and `evidence/SHA256SUMS`: `evidence/scorers/` (the tools; `evidence/scorers/historic/` holds the exact runner and scorer versions each chain log names), `evidence/families/`, `evidence/panel/`, `evidence/chains/`, `evidence/env/`, the per-experiment trees and `evidence/anchors/`. The kit root holds only `README.md`, `verify.py`, `make_bundle.py` (the authors' builder, for inspection). Run everything from the kit root, and put the virtual environment somewhere else (`python3 -m venv ~/auditkit-venv`) rather than inside the kit.

## The verification path (no GPU)
Three modes, from safest to most complete. Nothing in the static mode executes anything a model wrote.
```
python verify.py --static-only      # ~1 second, any OS: hashes + inventory, bills, table bindings, FP-688 arithmetic, tables
python verify.py                     # + step 3: evalplus re-executes the saved HumanEval+ programs (Linux / macOS / WSL; ~15-25 min)
python verify.py --regrade           # + step 4: the family graders re-execute every stored skill program (the only step that runs model-written code outside evalplus); this mode is a superset of the default one, so run it instead of the default, not after it
python verify.py --regrade --skip-anchors   # step 4 without the evalplus step (~1 minute)
```
Setup for the two executing modes: Linux, macOS or WSL (on WSL launch a Linux distribution with `python3`, e.g. `wsl -d Ubuntu`), with network for pip and for evalplus's first run, which downloads the HumanEval+ dataset and its ground-truth cache (about 260 MB):
```
cd <kit root>; python3 -m venv ~/auditkit-venv && . ~/auditkit-venv/bin/activate
pip install evalplus==0.3.1            # ~90 packages (it pulls transformers, datasets and three vendor SDKs); 5-15 minutes depending on disk and network
```
Honest timing from two cold tests on a Windows laptop with the kit on a WSL mount: first test (13 sample files) venv 44 s, pip 16 min, verify 9 min 54 s, total 27 minutes; second test (23 sample files) venv 34 s, pip 14 min 43 s, verify 20 min 31 s, total 36 minutes. Since then the rescoring deduplicates byte-identical sample files (23 files, 15 distinct), so the evalplus step is about a third shorter. Native Linux disks are faster. Use `python -u verify.py | tee run.log` if you redirect output; the script flushes every line but a pipe may still buffer.

**What the passing runs still do not establish.** No number in the kit is derived from weights: reads name their model by a weights sha (the runner receipts, the loss receipts, the manifests, now cross-checked by step 2d) but the kit never loads weights. A forger who rewrites every file consistently, including the raw log-probabilities, passes every static step; the executing steps then catch only a fabricated skill pass (step 4) and not a swapped capability file that is genuinely a different model's output. The `expect_identical` flags in step 2b are the authors' assertions about which arms are deterministic; pairs marked otherwise are reported, not enforced. Those are the reasons the root digest and the weights deposit are release items, not README items.

**What runs model-written code, and how.** Step 3 hands the saved programs (15 distinct HumanEval+ sample files, 164 programs each) to evalplus, which runs them in its own subprocess sandbox with time and memory limits, in a temporary directory. Step 4 (`--regrade`) runs each report's stored programs (1,200 in this bundle) through the family graders in a fresh subprocess with a 15-minute timeout, a scratch working directory and bytecode writing disabled; that is process isolation with your user's rights (on a default WSL install that user is root, with the Windows drives mounted under /mnt; use a non-root user or a container), not an OS sandbox, and the grader's own docstring says so (`vex_tasks.py`: runs that rely on it are "benchmark-development, not custody-grade evidence" until an OS-level sandbox lands). If you do not trust the bundle, run steps 3 and 4 inside a container or VM, or stay with `--static-only`. Neither step is reached if any hash or inventory check fails: the verifier stops before anything executable. The verifier writes nothing into the evidence tree (bytecode writing is disabled, evalplus runs on copies) and step 1 fails if any file is present that SHA256SUMS does not list.

**What `verify.py` checks.** (0) prints the sha256 of itself, this README, `bundle.json` and `SHA256SUMS` so a run can be bound to exact files (the sha of `SHA256SUMS` is the kit's root digest; it will be published with the DOI and the commit, and until then step 1 is self-referential: a bundle whose every file was rewritten consistently passes it); (1) every file against SHA256SUMS, every pre-registration against the sha quoted in `bundle.json`, and the inventory (file, arm and prereg counts recorded in `bundle.json`) met and nonzero, then stops on any failure; (2) every bill and crossing count recomputed from the saved base and candidate margins, with the same 641 ids on both sides and every margin finite, and every number in the tables bound to its file: teach and held-out counts to the cap reports (passed-row count equals the recorded count; unique task ids; split field consistent; row count equals task count), HumanEval+ to the scoring receipt and the samples sha, bill to the price file; (2b) the stored code of every re-read hashed against the first read's code shas; (2c) every one of the 14,102 margins recomputed from the stored per-candidate log-probabilities and the bank's gold answers, and every gain recomputed (a bill forged consistently across the margins, the table and the price file fails here unless the raw log-probabilities are forged too); (2d) each runner receipt's recorded read-file shas bound to the shipped files, its bill to the table, and its weights sha to the export manifest and to the FP-688 loss receipt that names the same weights; (3) evalplus rescoring; (4) skill regrading where code is stored; (4b) every FP-688 loss delta recomputed from base and candidate receipts read on the same text, tokens, torch and gpu, with the per-document deltas (unweighted mean and the count of documents the candidate is worse on) checked against the token-weighted number; (5) the tables, with a gain column beside every bill. Nonzero exit on any mismatch; the evidence tree is never written to. Tampered copies of the bundle (empty inventory, a NaN margin with its hash re-signed, an extra panel id, a duplicate skill row with a contradictory split, an unsigned byte flip) all fail or stop; those five cases are the validator's own regression tests and are described in the release notes.

**The two FP-678 tables, and what their agreement is.** The first run (2026-09-11) stored only the sha of each generated program. The same eight arms were rerun on 2026-09-12 on the same machine, stack and seed with the code-storing runner (`scorers/fp678_h2h_v2.py`, one field added) and appear as the second table. Step 2b hashes every stored program of the second run against the first run's recorded code shas: base, U_vex, P_coding, LoRA and full fine-tune 120 of 120 identical (the first table's teach column for those arms is therefore retroactively verified from stored code); GRACE 119 of 120 (one held-out program differs); WISE 2 of 120 and AlphaEdit 0 of 120 (their routing and closed-form updates are not run-to-run deterministic; WISE moved from teach 1 and HumanEval+ 99 to teach 2 and 98, AlphaEdit from bill 2038 / 117 crossings / HumanEval+ 0 to 1741 / 91 / 6). What this establishes: adding the field did not perturb generation, and the determinism of our writes and the fine-tunes on this stack. What it does not establish: replication in any sense an evaluator cares about; the agreeing arms are one measurement reported twice, there is no second seed, machine, stack or dose, and every screen number in the kit is n = 1. WISE's HumanEval+ drop from 112 to 99 with a bill of 0.000 is the head-to-head's own finding that the bill is blind to routed editors, restated by the rerun. The px3 row of the FP-682c table was also re-read with the code-storing runner (px3v2): 240 of 240 programs identical, so px3's teach counts are verified from stored code. The FP-682b rows (pq2, pq3) are still sha-only: their receipts are writer receipts without a reads block, so step 2d binds their weights sha only through the export manifest and, for pq3, the FP-688 loss receipt; the read files name the model by path. Their re-read with the code-storing runner is queued on box 2. One naming note: the span-projection bases file the writer names in its receipt is called `fp658_coding_bases.pt` for every span, a legacy of the tool that builds it; the bases receipt next to it (`pool_key PANEL641`, `n_prompts 641`, built from the 641 protected facts) says which span it holds, and its output sha equals the `bases_sha256` in the writer receipt.

**Versions.** `bundle.json` carries a `runs` list, one entry per `CHAIN START` line in the shipped chain logs, naming the runner, scorer, writer and chain shas that produced each piece of evidence, and a `pins_note`. The FP-678 first run used runner `0f32a0eb` and scorers `24883964` (v0), `a8213689` (v0.1) and `74e5513c` (v0.3), all under `evidence/scorers/historic/`; the pre-registration's frozen line names the v0 pair and its amendments v0.1-v0.3 (same file) name the later scorers and the reason for each. `scorers/fp678_h2h.py` is the FP-682c-era runner (`e570bce2`) and `scorers/fp678_h2h_v2.py` the code-storing runner (`de956806`). `evidence/chains/` holds every chain: `fp678_chain.sh` (v0, whose `rm -rf` destroyed the first U_vex export before its read, recorded in the log as `ARM U_vex FAILED`), `fp678b/c/d_chain.sh` (the replacements that produced the U_vex, P_coding and fine-tune evidence) and `fp678_chain_v2.sh`; the box-2 chain's helpers (`fp_supervisor.py`, `pilot_teach.py`, `paired_retention.py`) ship with it. The FP-682b tree ships its box-2 chain log as `fp682b/run.log`. The four `WSL SCORING FAILED` lines in `fp678/chain.log` are false alarms from the chain's grep-based success test: the scoring receipts for those arms exist and their stamps match the log lines to the second.

## What the checks do and do not establish
Established: the evidence files are the ones hashed and nothing else is present; every bill and crossing count is correctly computed from the saved margins over the same 641 ids with finite values; every table number is the number in the file it claims to come from; the HumanEval+ sample sets score exactly as their receipts say under evalplus 0.3.1; the stored programs of the re-reads regrade to their recorded counts and hash to the first reads' code shas as stated above; the FP-688 deltas are correctly computed from receipts read on the same box, text and stack. Not established by any step: the two means each FP-688 delta is built from (the receipts do not carry per-document token counts, so the token-weighted mean cannot be recomputed); the receipts' own claims about which weights produced which file (the 5090 arm receipts carry read-file shas and a weights sha, and those cross-links hold when checked by hand, but nothing in the kit re-derives a number from weights).

Not established by the kit alone: that a saved file came from the model it names (provenance rests on the receipts, which carry model shas, stack fields and stamps, and on the chain logs shipped in `fp678/`, `fp682b/`, `fp682c/`); that the pre-registrations preceded the reads (the ordering evidence is the git commit that added each file, listed in `bundle.json`, checkable against the public repository once it is public, plus the referee-channel packets that quote each prereg's sha before its results); that this kit is the kit (there is no external root digest yet: at release the sha256 of `SHA256SUMS` printed by step 0 will be published with the DOI and the commit, and until then step 1 is self-referential). The FP-682b teach counts (see the versions paragraph). The rerun path below re-establishes all of it from weights.

**Routed editors read as the base.** WISE and GRACE leave the base weights untouched and route edits through side memories keyed on the training prompts. Their panel margins are byte-identical to the base on all 641 facts and GRACE's HumanEval+ samples are byte-identical to the base's: the routers never fired on fact prompts or HumanEval prompts. That is the finding the head-to-head reports ("the panel is blind to routed editors; a capability anchor next to the panel is not optional"), not an artefact — and the only number that shows GRACE was live is its teach 5/60, which step 4 now regrades from the stored code in the v2 run.

**"Gated" in this bundle.** The gate is the pre-registered three-screen pass/fail applied after the write; the in-write guard is the projection out of the protected span. The writer's refusal machinery (per-step protected-check with rollback) exists and did not fire in these arms: `P_coding`'s writer receipt records 113 of 113 steps accepted and no guard check in this mode. Nothing here shows a refused write.

## Three hours, one 24-32 GB GPU: the rerun path
`scorers/` holds the writer (`fp667_family_writer.py` + `decision_guard/`), the runner (`fp678_h2h.py`, which reads teach / held-out / panel / anchor in one process and checks its own reads row-for-row against the pinned instruments `cap_eval.py` and `panel_read_hf.py`), the price tool, the span builder (`fp658_coding_bases.py`) and the scorers written before any read. `families/` holds the lessons, the graders, the replay pools and the panel-span source; `panel/` the 641 facts and the candidate bank; `chains/` the exact chain scripts that produced the evidence; `env/` the `pip freeze` of the three environments (5090 reads/editors/FT, 5090 writer, box-2 writer/reads). The two artifacts (S1 to px3 lineage; pq3) ship as weights with the release deposit. The release item that follows this kit, asked for by its second cold tester, is a containerised one-arm reproduction from weights: a digest-pinned image, the P_coding or pq3 weights fetched by sha, and one command that regenerates that arm's three screen numbers on the reader's own GPU; run twice with different seeds it would be the first replication in the record rather than a determinism replay.

## What is not claimed
One model family in the head-to-head (Qwen2.5-3B; 1.5B and 7B installer points exist, 14B and two other families are running at release time, and one of those, SmolLM2-1.7B, did not install at this dose); one seed per lineage; the editors ran at their published fact-lane hyper-parameters, not re-tuned for skills; synthetic skill families; the projection geometry is standard (Adam-NSCL / GPM / OWM, CrispEdit) — the certification, the price and the receipts are the contribution. "Pretraining-like text" in FP-688 means 200 documents of NeelNanda/pile-10k, 512 tokens each.

## Housekeeping disclosures
The receipts and chain logs carry the authors' machine paths (a OneDrive folder on Windows, a home directory on the DGX box) because they are hashed as produced; scrubbing them would change the hashes. `make_bundle.py` is the authors' bundle builder and hard-codes those paths; it is shipped for inspection, not for running elsewhere. `bundle.json` timestamps are local time on the authors' machines.
